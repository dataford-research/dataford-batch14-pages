The Worst Interview Companies in 2026

By Amney Mounir

At Canonical, 63% of candidates rate the interview negative and only 8% get an offer. No other company is within 15 points. Airwallex (47%) and Oscar Health (45%) follow. High pay does not buy a good process: most of the list is well-funded software.

Dataford ranked 25 employers with 200 or more rated reports by negative-experience share in The 25 Worst Interview Companies in 2026, using 800k+ interview reports.

Who sits at the top

  • Canonical leads at 63% negative, 12% positive and an 8% offer rate across about 520 reports.
  • Airwallex is second at 47% negative with about $223k median pay.
  • Oscar Health is third at 45% negative.

Well-funded software dominates

Sixteen of the 25 pay a median of $180k or more. The list includes Cloudflare, Okta, Navan, Braze, GitHub, Gusto, Rippling, Asana and Confluent. Anthropic is #20 at 38% negative with the highest pay on the list, about $555k, and a 4% offer rate. Rippling, Asana and Navan follow on pay.

Hard is not the main pattern

Only Canonical (3.44) and Anthropic (3.28) sit clearly above average difficulty. Vertafore (2.73) and BILL (2.77) are easier than average and still land here. GitHub has the highest complaint rate on the list at 30%, mostly ghosting. Bumble (26%), Navan (24%) and Airwallex (23%) follow.

How I would act on this

  1. Treat a high negative rate as a process warning, especially when offer rates are also low.
  2. Do not assume pay will make a rough loop feel fine.
  3. Read recent candidate notes for ghosting and feedback gaps before you invest a week.
  4. Keep higher-experience employers on the same slate so one bad process does not freeze the search.

Candidate write-ups on Dataford's interview experiences page help you see the pattern before you schedule.

A rough process is information. Use it to set expectations and backups, then decide with eyes open.

Canonical's difficulty is hard at 3.44, which helps explain some of the pain, but Airwallex sits near average difficulty at 2.97 with a 47% negative rate. Process quality and question difficulty are not the same variable.

When sixteen of twenty-five employers still clear $180k, the list is a warning for candidates who assume compensation buys a clean loop. It does not. Feedback gaps and ghosting show up in well-funded software as often as anywhere else.

If a company on this list is still a top choice for the work or the pay, budget extra time for ambiguity and keep a parallel process that converts faster. The data is about base rates, not about forbidding the application.
