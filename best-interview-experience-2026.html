Companies With the Best Interview Experience in 2026

By Amney Mounir

A good interview is one candidates leave calling positive. In Dataford's ranking of companies with 300 or more rated reports, Sandia National Laboratories leads at 80% positive and 9% negative. Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory is next at 79%, then TechnipFMC at 78%.

The full list of 25 is in The 25 Companies With the Best Interview Experience in 2026, built from 800k+ interview reports.

National labs and defense fill the list

  • Eleven of the 25 seats go to national labs, defense and aerospace employers.
  • Sandia, Johns Hopkins APL, Lockheed Martin, RTX, KBR, Northrop Grumman, GE Aerospace and Collins Aerospace all appear.
  • Only one classic Big Tech name makes it: Dell at 77% positive. Intel is at 74%. Google, Meta, Amazon and Apple do not.

Good experience usually comes with a decision

Twenty of the 25 have offer rates above 50%. Universal Orlando converts 77%, Nordstrom 75% and The Johns Hopkins University 74%. Candidates leave with an answer, not silence. Lockheed Martin also has the lowest negative rate on the list at 6%.

Pay is solid but not frontier lab money

Protiviti and Nordstrom are the best paid on the list, about $185k and $189k. Most of the list sits between $100k and $175k. Sandia posts about $161k with a 4.5 work-life rating. The filter here is experience quality, not maximum cash.

How I would use this

  1. If you want a calmer process, start with employers that already clear a high positive rate.
  2. Read the offer column next to the experience score so you know whether a yes is common.
  3. Do not treat Big Tech as the default for a respectful loop. The data says otherwise.
  4. Ask recent candidates how many rounds they sat and how feedback arrived.

Recent write-ups on Dataford's interview experiences page show what those loops feel like before you apply.

A high positive rate is rare. When you find one that also matches your role and pay bar, put it near the top of the slate.

Sandia's offer rate is 62% with average difficulty around 2.85. Johns Hopkins APL sits near 2.73 difficulty with a $172.5k median. TechnipFMC posts about $104k with a 61% offer rate. The pattern is consistent: respectful processes and a clear yes or no.

Allegiant Air also appears in the defense and travel cluster that fills so many seats. When national labs and aerospace take nearly half the list, it is a reminder that brand-name consumer tech is not where candidates report the best days.

I treat this ranking as a shortlist filter for people who burn out on chaotic loops. Pay will often sit below frontier lab numbers, and that is fine if the process itself is the constraint you are optimizing.
